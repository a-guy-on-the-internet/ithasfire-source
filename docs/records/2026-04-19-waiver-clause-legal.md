## Key Findings

### Tennessee Framework

- **Copeland v. HealthSouth, 565 S.W.3d 260 (Tenn. 2018):** Three-factor test (bargaining power, clarity, public policy). Replaced rigid Olson six-factor test. Struck down overbroad hospital-transport waiver.
- **Empress Health & Beauty Spa v. Turner, 503 S.W.2d 188 (Tenn. 1973):** "Negligence" is not a magic word in TN. But use it anyway for multistate safety.
- **Adams v. Roark, 686 S.W.2d 73 (Tenn. 1985):** Gross negligence carve-out mandatory. Overbroad clauses attempting to waive it can void entire document.
- **Perez v. McConkey, 872 S.W.2d 897 (Tenn. 1994):** Implied assumption of risk abolished. Express assumption of risk preserved as complete bar.
- **Henderson v. Quest Expeditions, 174 S.W.3d 730 (Tenn. Ct. App. 2005):** Broadly worded inherent-risk language enforced even for off-activity injuries.
- **Hudson v. Gaitan, 675 S.W.2d 699 (Tenn. 1984):** Abolished invitee/licensee distinction. Duty is "reasonable care under all attendant circumstances."
- **Childress v. Madison County, 777 S.W.2d 1 (Tenn. Ct. App. 1989):** Parent cannot waive minor's tort claims.
- **Blackwell v. Sky High Sports Nashville, 523 S.W.3d 624 (Tenn. Ct. App. 2017):** Reaffirmed Childress, rejected forum-selection clause designed to evade it.

### Release Language

- Stack all synonyms: "release, waive, discharge, and covenant not to sue"
- "Hold harmless" and "indemnify" synonymous in TN (Long v. McAllister-Long)
- Released Parties must be specifically enumerated — vague "all related parties" invites narrowing under contra proferentem
- Always carve out gross negligence, recklessness, intentional misconduct, fraud, and statutory violations

### Assumption of Risk

- Must be express (implied abolished by Perez v. McConkey)
- "Including but not limited to" catch-all accepted and preferred (avoids ejusdem generis narrowing)
- Enumerate specific risks relevant to event type

### Premises Liability

- TN abolished invitee/licensee distinction (Hudson v. Gaitan)
- Charging admission eliminates Recreational Use Statute immunity (T.C.A. §§ 70-7-101 to -105)
- Ghost Ship: $32.7M city settlement, $60M+ total, 36 counts involuntary manslaughter. Waivers cannot protect against fire code violations or criminal conduct.
- Enumerate residential hazards: stairs, handrails, lighting, egress, sprinklers, pets

### Amplified Sound

- Concert hearing claims almost always fail on open-and-obvious grounds
- Powell v. Fogerty (NY 1998): dismissed, loud music damage "perfectly obvious"
- Goldscheider v. Royal Opera House (UK 2019): liability found but workplace case, not patron
- Don't cite OSHA dB numbers in waiver (gives plaintiffs a standard to argue)
- Industry standard: warn about high dB levels, recommend hearing protection
- Live Nation ticket-back language is the template

### Alcohol

- TN dram shop (T.C.A. § 57-10-101): consumption is proximate cause, not furnishing
- § 57-10-102: beyond-reasonable-doubt standard (criminal standard in civil case)
- Biscan v. Brown (Tenn. 2005): host liable for knowingly permitting minor drinking
- Ennabe v. Manosa (Cal. 2014): cover charge = "sale" of alcohol, strips social-host immunity
- TN hasn't adopted Ennabe but risk exists
- BYOB waiver clause is informational for first-party claims; cannot waive third-party claims by injured strangers
- Indemnification from attendees for alcohol-related third-party claims is theoretically valid but practically worthless (judgment-proof attendees)

### Outdoor Events

- Enumerate specific weather risks (lightning, heat stroke, hypothermia, dehydration)
- Indiana State Fair collapse (2011): $50M settlements, Act of God defense failed because storm was forecast
- Weather monitoring plan should be referenced in the waiver

### Fire and Pyrotechnics

- TN requires State Fire Marshal permit (T.C.A. § 68-104-211) for outdoor public displays
- NFPA 1123 (outdoor) and NFPA 1126 (proximate audience) incorporated by reference
- Certified operator required
- Waiver cannot substitute for permit — can actually prove knowledge in prosecution
- Astroworld (2021): 10 killed, $280M in litigation spending, all wrongful-death suits settled
- Post-Astroworld: waivers must include crowd-density acknowledgment, show-stop compliance, evacuation compliance

### Photo/Media Release

- Separate legal instrument from liability waiver (license + privacy release, not injury waiver)
- Tennessee ELVIS Act (2024): adds "voice" to right of publicity, creates AI deepfake secondary liability
- Cover voice explicitly, exclude AI synthetic use
- GDPR: photos are personal data; facial recognition is Art. 9 biometric data requiring explicit consent
- Illinois BIPA: $1,000-$5,000 per violation for faceprint capture without written consent
- COPPA: verifiable parental consent for under-13

### Medical Acknowledgment

- Emergency consent clause has limited independent legal force for conscious adults
- Real work is for minors — advance parental consent under T.C.A. § 63-6-222
- TN Good Samaritan Law (§ 63-6-218) is unusually broad — covers anyone rendering emergency care at events with or without admission charge
- AVOID creating voluntary-undertaking duty: say organizer "may but is not obligated to" arrange medical response
- Don't promise on-site medical unless you'll actually provide it (Restatement § 324A)

### Governing Law / Forum Selection

- TN forum-selection clauses presumptively valid (Dyersburg Machine Works, 1983)
- Carnival Cruise Lines v. Shute (SCOTUS 1991): non-negotiated forum clause on cruise ticket upheld
- Arbitration: generally strong but Heckman v. Live Nation (9th Cir. 2024) struck bellwether protocol as unconscionable
- Current best practice: individual arbitration under AAA Consumer Rules with batching (not bellwether) protocol

### Severability

- TN uses modified blue-pencil approach (Murfreesboro Medical Clinic v. Udom, 2005)
- Direct courts to reform rather than void: "modified, narrowed, and blue-penciled to minimum extent"
- Fails when entire scheme violates public policy

### Minors

- ~25 states reject parental pre-injury waivers (including TN, NJ, FL commercial, KY, WA, UT, MI, ME, IA, CT, PA, IL, MO, OK, NY, WI)
- ~12 states enforce them (CO by statute, OH, MD, MA, CA, MN, ND, AK, DE, IN, NC limited)
- TN: Childress (1989) + Blackwell (2017) = parent cannot waive minor's claims
- Narrow parental indemnity (for parent's own claims + minor's conduct causing third-party harm) may survive

### Clickwrap

- Browsewrap dead: Nguyen v. Barnes & Noble (9th Cir. 2014)
- Sign-in-wrap upheld: Meyer v. Uber (2d Cir. 2017) — but Cullinane v. Uber (1st Cir. 2018) rejected gray hyperlink (must be blue/underlined)
- For personal injury waivers: use hybrid scrollwrap + clickwrap (dedicated step, unchecked checkbox)
- Pre-checked boxes violate CCPA dark-pattern rules and GDPR Art. 4(11)
- ESIGN (15 U.S.C. §§ 7001+) and TN UETA (T.C.A. §§ 47-10-101+) validate electronic signatures
- Log: user ID, email, IP, UTC timestamp (ms), waiver body hash, rendered HTML, user-agent, session ID
- Retain 6 years (TN contract SOL § 28-3-109), minors: age 18 + SOL

### Platform Liability / UPL

- Providing waiver templates is "self-help" per Dacey v. N.Y. County Lawyers' Ass'n (1967)
- LegalZoom's interactive decision-tree model was challenged as UPL (Janson v. LegalZoom, 2011; NC consent judgment 2015)
- TN UPL: T.C.A. § 23-3-103, Class A misdemeanor, $10K/violation
- Position as informational templates, not legal advice
- Organizer is the drafter for contra proferentem
- All competitors disclaim aggressively (Smartwaiver, WaiverForever, Jotform, DocuSign)
- Annual TN attorney review of clause library is the critical investment ($1-2K)
