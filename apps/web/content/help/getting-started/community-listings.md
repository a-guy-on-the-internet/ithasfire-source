Some events on Ithas Fire aren't run by a single organizer at all. **Community listings** are free, Wikipedia-style event pages — open mics, jams, markets, meetups — that the community keeps accurate together. They appear in search, browse, and the map right alongside ticketed events.

## What makes a community listing different

- **No tickets, ever.** Community listings are free. There's no checkout, pricing, or resale — instead of "Get tickets" you'll see an **I'm going** button.
- **No single owner.** The page shows a **Community-maintained listing** badge instead of an organizer. Anyone can help keep the details correct.
- **Moderated changes.** Every suggested change is reviewed by a community moderator before it goes live, so the page you see has always been checked by a person.

## RSVP with "I'm going"

On a community listing, tap **I'm going** to mark that you plan to attend. Tap it again any time to change your mind.

- You need to be signed in — if you're not, the button takes you to sign-in and brings you straight back.
- Your RSVP is **completely private**. There is no public attendee count or list; the button only ever reflects your own state.
- When you RSVP, you'll get a confirmation — an in-app notification and an email with the event details and a link back to the event.

### Where to see your RSVPs

Everything you've said **I'm going** to is listed on your [My RSVPs](/my-rsvps) page — open the account menu and choose **My RSVPs** under **Going**. The list stays private to you, and each event links straight back to its page. To cancel an RSVP, tap **I'm going** again on the event page — you can get there from My RSVPs via **View event**.

:::tip
RSVP is separate from saving an event. **Save** keeps any event on your private watchlist; **I'm going** says you plan to attend a free community listing. See [Saving events](/help/account/saving-events).
:::

## Recurring dates

Some community listings — a weekly open mic, a monthly market — repeat on a schedule. When a moderator creates a listing, they can turn on **Repeats** and choose how often it runs: **Weekly** or **Monthly**, which day (or days) it lands on, how many weeks or months to skip between dates, and a **Repeat until** end date.

A recurring listing is still a single page. Instead of one date, it opens with how often the event runs — "Every Wednesday, 8:00 PM" — then the next date, then the handful of dates after that. **Show more dates** opens the rest of the schedule, grouped by month. In search and browse, its card carries a small **Recurring** label so you can spot it at a glance.

You RSVP to a **specific date**, not the whole series. The next date has an **I'm going** button, and each later date is a button too — tap the one you plan to attend, and tap it again to change your mind. As with any RSVP, your choice is private: there's no attendee count, and a button only ever reflects your own state.

:::tip
Only moderators set or change the repeat schedule. Recurrence isn't part of the **Suggest an edit** flow below — you can still suggest fixes to the title, description, or location, but the schedule of a recurring listing is moderator-managed.
:::

## Photos

A community listing can carry a **banner image** at the top of its page, which also appears on its card in search and browse. There are two ways one gets added:

- A moderator can add it after creating the listing: **Continue to editor** opens the full editor, which has an optional **Hero Image** field.
- Anyone can propose one later through **Suggest an edit** (see below). Attaching a photo to a suggestion is the one part of that flow that needs you to be signed in.

Either way, a photo only appears once it's live — a moderator-added banner shows immediately; a suggested one appears after the suggestion is reviewed.

## Suggest an edit

Spotted a wrong start time, a moved place, or a typo on a community listing? You can suggest an edit on any published community listing:

1. Open the listing's page and choose **Suggest edit**, next to the line naming the listing as community-maintained.
2. Change the fields that need fixing — things like the title, description, date and time, or location.
3. Submit. Your suggestion goes into a review queue; it never changes the page directly.

You **don't need an account** to suggest an edit — the one exception is attaching a photo (see **Photos** above), which needs you signed in. Suggestions are reviewed by community moderators before anything changes.

Suggested edits are only available on community listings. Events run by an organizer on the platform are managed by that organizer directly — if you spot a problem on one, reach out to the organizer from their page.

## Report a listing

Suggesting an edit fixes the details on a community event listing. **Report** is for a different job: flagging a place's page, or a person's or organization's page, that has a bigger problem — it's closed or gone, the information is wrong, it duplicates another listing, it's impersonating someone, or it's inappropriate or spam.

1. Open the page and choose **Report** from the **…** menu, at the end of the row of buttons under the page's name.
2. Reporting needs an account. If you're signed out, pressing **Report** takes you to sign-in first; once you're back on the page, press it again.
3. Pick a reason. Place pages offer **Closed or gone**, **Incorrect info**, **Duplicate**, **Inappropriate**, **Spam**, and **Other**; pages for people and organizations offer **Impersonation**, **Incorrect info**, **Inappropriate**, **Spam**, and **Other**.
4. Add details if you have them — anything specific helps the review. Details are optional unless your reason is **Other**, which requires them.
5. Choose **Submit report**.

Your report goes to the platform's staff, who resolve or dismiss it, and you'll get an in-app notification either way once it's been reviewed. You can only have one open report per listing: if you've already reported it and that report is still waiting for review, the dialog says so instead of filing a second one.

:::tip
Three tools, three jobs. **Suggest an edit** fixes details on a community event listing. **Report** flags a place's or a person's or organization's page for the platform's staff. And a problem on an organizer-run event goes to the organizer — reach out from their page.
:::

## Become a community moderator

Community moderators review suggested edits and create new community listings. If you'd like to help:

1. Open any community listing and find the **Help keep community listings accurate** card.
2. Choose **Apply to moderate** and (optionally) tell us why you'd like to help.
3. A platform admin reviews your application — you'll be able to moderate once it's approved.

You can only have one application open at a time.

## For moderators: set a listing's location

When you create or edit a community listing, **Location** is one search box. Type a name or an address and the results come back in two groups:

- **On Ithas Fire**: places that already have a page here. Pick one and the listing links to that place: it appears on the place's page, and the address, map pin and time zone come from the place.
- **Addresses**: street addresses and businesses from the map search. Pick one and the listing gets that address and a pin, but no place page.

Use the arrow keys to move through both groups, **Enter** to pick, and **Escape** to close the list. Pick a place whenever there is one, so every listing there shows up on one page.

### Add a place

If the place isn't on Ithas Fire yet, choose **Add a place** at the bottom of the results. (When nothing matched, it reads **Add "…" as a place** with what you typed.) A dialog opens over your listing, so nothing you've entered is lost:

1. **Name**: filled with what you typed. If you pick a business as the address and the name is empty, its name fills in.
2. **Address**: pick the address from the suggestions so the place gets a map pin. Check the pin preview sits on the building.
3. **Unit or room** (optional): a suite, unit or back room, such as "Unit A". It's added to the place's address.
4. Choose **Add place**. The new place is picked for your listing straight away and shows in the places directory. It has no owner until someone claims it.

If you picked an address and want it to become a place, choose **Make this a place** under it to open the same dialog with the address filled in.

If a place with the same name already exists at that address, the dialog shows it instead. Choose **Use this place** to link your listing to it, so listings there share one page.

### Fix or archive a place

With a place picked, choose **Edit place** to fix its name or address. Changes show on every listing at that place. To add a unit or room while editing, pick the address again first.

Some places can't be changed here, and the field tells you why before any form opens: the place has an owner, someone has asked to own it, an organizer's upcoming event uses it, or it keeps its address private.

**Archive place**, at the bottom of the edit dialog, takes a place out of place search and the picker. Past listings keep showing it. A place with upcoming listings can't be archived until they end or move to another place; the dialog says how many there are. If a listing's place has been archived, the field says so, and you'll need to pick another place.

Archived a place by mistake? Ask a platform admin: they can restore it from **Platform → Places**. A restored place comes back to place search and the picker, and to the places directory only if it was listed there before.

:::warning
Adding, changing or archiving a place needs a second factor on your account: a passkey or an authenticator app. If you don't have one, the field shows a **Set up a second factor** link that opens in a new tab, so the listing you're working on stays open. Set it up, come back, and try again.
:::
