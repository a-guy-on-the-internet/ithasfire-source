Some events on Ithas Fire aren't run by a single organizer at all. **Community listings** are free, Wikipedia-style event pages — open mics, jams, markets, meetups — that the community keeps accurate together. They appear in search, browse, and the map right alongside ticketed events.

## What makes a community listing different

- **No tickets, ever.** Community listings are free. There's no checkout, pricing, or resale — instead of "Get tickets" you'll see an **I'm going** button.
- **No single owner.** The page shows a **Community-maintained listing** badge instead of an organizer. Anyone can help keep the details correct.
- **Moderated changes.** Every suggested change is reviewed by a community moderator before it goes live, so the page you see has always been checked by a person.

## RSVP with "I'm going"

On a community listing, tap **I'm going** to mark that you plan to attend. Tap it again any time to change your mind.

- You need to be signed in — if you're not, the button takes you to sign-in and brings you straight back.
- Your RSVP is **completely private**. There is no public attendee count or list; the button only ever reflects your own state.
- When you RSVP, you'll get a confirmation — an in-app notification and an email with the event details and a link back to the event.

### Where to see your RSVPs

Everything you've said **I'm going** to is listed on your [My RSVPs](/my-rsvps) page — open the account menu and choose **My RSVPs** under **Attend**. The list stays private to you, and each event links straight back to its page. To cancel an RSVP, tap **I'm going** again on the event page — you can get there from My RSVPs via **View event**.

:::tip
RSVP is separate from saving an event. **Save** keeps any event on your private watchlist; **I'm going** says you plan to attend a free community listing. See [Saving events](/help/account/saving-events).
:::

## Recurring dates

Some community listings — a weekly open mic, a monthly market — repeat on a schedule. When a moderator creates a listing, they can turn on **Repeats** and choose how often it runs: **Weekly** or **Monthly**, which day (or days) it lands on, how many weeks or months to skip between dates, and a **Repeat until** end date.

A recurring listing is still a single page. Instead of one date, it shows an **Upcoming dates** list, and in search and browse its card carries a small **Recurring** label so you can spot it at a glance.

You RSVP to a **specific date**, not the whole series. Each date in the list has its own **I'm going** toggle — tap the one you plan to attend, and tap it again to change your mind. As with any RSVP, your choice is private: there's no attendee count, and the toggle only ever reflects your own state.

:::tip
Only moderators set or change the repeat schedule. Recurrence isn't part of the **Suggest an edit** flow below — you can still suggest fixes to the title, description, or location, but the schedule of a recurring listing is moderator-managed.
:::

## Photos

A community listing can carry a **banner image** at the top of its page, which also appears on its card in search and browse. There are two ways one gets added:

- A moderator can add it while **creating** the listing — the create form has an optional **Banner image** field.
- Anyone can propose one later through **Suggest an edit** (see below). Attaching a photo to a suggestion is the one part of that flow that needs you to be signed in.

Either way, a photo only appears once it's live — a moderator-added banner shows immediately; a suggested one appears after the suggestion is reviewed.

## Suggest an edit

Spotted a wrong start time, a moved venue, or a typo on a community listing? You can suggest an edit on any published community listing:

1. Open the listing's page and choose **Suggest an edit** in the action bar at the bottom.
2. Change the fields that need fixing — things like the title, description, date and time, or location.
3. Submit. Your suggestion goes into a review queue; it never changes the page directly.

You **don't need an account** to suggest an edit — the one exception is attaching a photo (see **Photos** above), which needs you signed in. Suggestions are reviewed by community moderators before anything changes.

Suggested edits are only available on community listings. Events run by an organizer on the platform are managed by that organizer directly — if you spot a problem on one, reach out to the organizer from their page.

## Report a listing

Suggesting an edit fixes the details on a community event listing. **Report** is for a different job: flagging a venue page, or a person's or organization's page, that has a bigger problem — it's closed or gone, the information is wrong, it duplicates another listing, it's impersonating someone, or it's inappropriate or spam.

1. Open the page and choose **Report** in the action bar — if you don't see it, it's in the **…** menu.
2. Reporting needs an account. If you're signed out, pressing **Report** takes you to sign-in first; once you're back on the page, press it again.
3. Pick a reason. Venue pages offer **Closed or gone**, **Incorrect info**, **Duplicate**, **Inappropriate**, **Spam**, and **Other**; pages for people and organizations offer **Impersonation**, **Incorrect info**, **Inappropriate**, **Spam**, and **Other**.
4. Add details if you have them — anything specific helps the review. Details are optional unless your reason is **Other**, which requires them.
5. Choose **Submit report**.

Your report goes to the platform's staff, who resolve or dismiss it, and you'll get an in-app notification either way once it's been reviewed. You can only have one open report per listing: if you've already reported it and that report is still waiting for review, the dialog says so instead of filing a second one.

:::tip
Three tools, three jobs. **Suggest an edit** fixes details on a community event listing. **Report** flags a venue's or a person's or organization's page for the platform's staff. And a problem on an organizer-run event goes to the organizer — reach out from their page.
:::

## Become a community moderator

Community moderators review suggested edits and create new community listings. If you'd like to help:

1. Open any community listing and find the **Help keep community listings accurate** card.
2. Choose **Apply to moderate** and (optionally) tell us why you'd like to help.
3. A platform admin reviews your application — you'll be able to moderate once it's approved.

You can only have one application open at a time.
