Ithas Fire searches events, people, groups, and places in one search. This article covers the search page — what each control does and which ones affect which results.

## Browse

The [homepage](/) opens with a search box — select it to open the search overlay — and a row of links to categories that have an upcoming event. **House shows** is in the footer. Below that, a ticker of upcoming shows runs across the page, followed by the **Upcoming events** section, where each card shows the title, place, date, and starting ticket price.

You can also open [/search](/search) with no search term at all. It lists everything matching your filters, so it works as a browse page as well as a search page.

## Search

Use the search bar at the top of any page, or go straight to [/search](/search). Results update as you type. You can search for:

- Event or artist name
- Place name
- City or postcode
- Category keywords — music, comedy, community, and so on

## Narrow by type

A row of buttons under the search bar picks what you are looking for:

**All** · **Events** · **People** · **Groups** · **Places**

**All** mixes every type together. The other four narrow the page to one kind of result, and the row works with arrow keys as well as clicks.

Category, date, and price only apply to events. Pick **People**, **Groups**, or **Places** and those controls step aside, because a place has no ticket price and a person has no start date. **Location** keeps working in every type.

## Filters

On a wide screen the filters sit in a column down the left, always visible. On a narrow screen they move behind a **Filters** button that opens a panel from the bottom of the screen.

The four groups, in order:

- **Category** — tick as many genres as you like
- **Date range** — one-tap **Today**, **This weekend**, **Next 7 days**, or **This month**, or set your own **From** and **To** dates
- **Price** — a **Free only** toggle, or **Min ($)** and **Max ($)** bounds. Turning **Free only** on disables the range, since the two would contradict each other
- **Location** — **Use my location**, or type a city or postcode, then set a **Radius**

The two screen sizes commit your changes differently. On a wide screen every change applies straight away. On a narrow screen your edits are held until you press **Show results**, which counts what you will get — so you can set up several filters without the list moving under your thumb.

Filters you have applied appear as buttons above the results. Press one to remove that filter on its own, or **Clear all** to start over.

:::tip
Closing the filter panel without pressing **Show results** keeps your edits. Reopen it and they are still there, waiting.
:::

## Sort

The **Sort** menu sits beside the result count:

- **Most relevant** — the default
- **Date: soonest** and **Date: latest**
- **Nearest first** — only offered once you have set a location, since there is nothing to measure from without one
- **Price: low to high** and **Price: high to low**
- **Most popular**

Sorting applies to events, so choosing anything other than **Most relevant** narrows the page to **Events**. Ordering a mixed list by price or start date would push every place and person to the bottom, which reads as though the search lost them.

Events with no ticket price on file sort last in both price directions. An event without a price is unknown rather than free, so it does not lead the cheapest-first list ahead of genuinely free events.

## Map

When results have locations, a **List** and **Map** toggle appears. On a wide screen the map sits beside the results. Pan or zoom it and a **Map area** button offers to search the part of the map you are looking at. Selecting a pin highlights that event in the list.

## Sharing a search

Your search term, the type you picked, your categories, and your location all live in the web address. Bookmark or share the link and it opens on the same results. Sort is not part of the address yet, so a shared link opens on **Most relevant**.

## Location permissions

**Use my location** asks your browser for permission once. Decline it and you can still type a city or postcode by hand — the radius works the same either way. If your browser has blocked location for the site, the button reports that it cannot get a fix rather than failing silently.

## What can go wrong

**No results at all.** Filters stack, so a narrow category plus a short date range plus a small radius can rule out everything. The filter buttons above the results show what is currently applied — remove them one at a time to find the one doing the damage.

**Results look wrong after using Back.** Filters and location are restored from the address, but sort is not, so it returns to **Most relevant**.
