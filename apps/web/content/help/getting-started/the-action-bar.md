The action bar is the dark bar that floats at the edge of the window and follows you as you scroll. It carries the main actions for whatever page you're on — **Get tickets** on an event, **Volunteer** where [an organiser is recruiting](/help/getting-started/volunteer-at-an-event), **Suggest an edit** on a [community listing](/help/getting-started/community-listings), **Report** on a venue's or a person's or organisation's page, **Create event** in your dashboard.

Its contents change with the page, so there's nothing to set up. This article covers the two things that aren't obvious: driving it from the keyboard, and moving it somewhere less annoying.

## What's in the bar

The bar shows as many actions as fit. When the window is too narrow for all of them, the least important ones collapse into a **…** button on the right — open it to reach the rest. Widen the window and they come back out on their own.

If a page has no actions to offer, the bar takes itself off screen rather than sitting there empty.

One action worth knowing by name: **Report**, on venue pages and the pages of people and organisations, flags a listing that has a real problem — closed, wrong, impersonating someone, inappropriate, or spam. It's usually tucked in the **…** menu, and it asks you to sign in if you aren't already. [Community listings and suggesting edits](/help/getting-started/community-listings) covers what happens after you press it.

## Keyboard shortcuts

On a desktop browser, every button in the bar answers to a number key. They run left to right: **1** is the leftmost button, **2** the next, and so on up to **9**. If the **…** button is showing, it takes the next number after the last visible action.

Hover over any button — or reach it with the Tab key — and a small key cap appears above it showing that button's number. That's the quickest way to learn the ones you use often.

:::tip
The numbers follow what's actually on screen, so they shift as the bar's contents change from page to page. Hover to check rather than memorising a number across different pages.
:::

Shortcuts stay out of your way: nothing fires while you're typing in a form field, while a dialog is open, or while the **…** menu itself is open. Numbers pressed with Ctrl, Cmd, or Alt held are left to your browser.

## Move the bar

If the bar covers something you're reading, drag it by the flame square at its left end. It snaps to one of seven resting places: five along the bottom of the window — left, centre, right, and two positions in between — plus the top-left and top-right corners.

You can also move it without a mouse. Tab to the flame square, then use the arrow keys: left and right step along the bottom row, up parks it in a top corner, and down brings it back.

Wherever you leave it, that's where it stays — the position is remembered in that browser and survives a reload. Dragging it also does nothing else: on a desktop the flame is only a handle, so use the logo in the top bar to get home.

## On a phone

The bar sits across the bottom of the screen on a phone and can't be moved or driven by number keys — there's no keyboard to drive it with and no room to move it to. Everything else works the same, including the **…** button when there are more actions than fit.

:::tip
Organiser pages swap the action bar for a bottom tab bar on phones and narrow windows, so you never get two bars stacked on top of each other. Widen the window and the action bar returns.
:::
