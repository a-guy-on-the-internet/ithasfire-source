A venue page can exist on Ithas Fire before its operator ever signs up — anyone can create a basic listing, and events can point at it. If you run the venue, claiming its page lets you correct its details, get it into the public directory, and manage it under your own account or organisation.

## Before you start

- You need an Ithas Fire account and must be signed in.
- Decide who should own the page: **you personally**, or an **organisation** you administer. You can only claim on behalf of an organisation where you're an admin.
- Have a way to prove you operate the venue ready — access to an email address at the venue's website domain, or a document such as a utility bill, lease, or business registration.

## Claiming a venue

1. Open the venue's page. If it's claimable, you'll see a **Claim this venue** button.
2. Choose who is claiming — **Myself**, or an organisation you administer.
3. Choose how to prove you operate the venue. Three methods are offered:
   - **Account email** — the domain of your verified account email matches the venue's website (for example, your account email ends in `@thecornertavern.com` and the venue's website is `thecornertavern.com`).
   - **Email code** — we send a six-digit code to an address you enter at the venue's website domain. Enter the code to confirm you control a mailbox there.
   - **Upload document** — attach one to five files (PDF, JPEG, or PNG, up to 10 MB each) that show you operate the venue.
4. Submit. You'll either see **Claim submitted** (your claim is now waiting for review) or, in the fast-track case described below, **You now own this venue**.

:::tip
Free and public email providers (Gmail, Outlook, and similar) can't be used for the **Account email** or **Email code** methods — they don't prove any link to the venue. Use an address at the venue's own domain, or choose the document method.
:::

### When a claim verifies instantly

If your **Account email** domain matches a venue website that we imported from a trusted source, the claim skips review and verifies immediately — the wizard confirms **You now own this venue**. Every other route (an email code, an uploaded document, or a website that was added by an unverified listing) stays pending until our team reviews it. This is deliberate: a venue's website can be set by whoever first created the page, so a domain match alone is a signal, not proof.

## While your claim is pending

A pending claim records that you've claimed the venue, but it does **not** yet let you edit the venue's details, and the venue stays out of the public directory until it's verified. The venue page shows a **Claim pending review** badge in the meantime.

To track a claim you've submitted, open your account settings and find the **Venue claims** section. Each claim shows its status — **Pending**, **In review**, **Approved**, or **Rejected** — along with the date it was reviewed and any note the reviewer left.

When a reviewer **approves** your claim, the venue becomes verified: you can edit its details and it's eligible for the public directory. If a claim is **rejected**, the venue returns to its unclaimed state and the reviewer's note explains why — you're free to correct the issue and claim again.

:::warning
You can hold up to 20 pending claims at once. Submit claims only for venues you actually operate; claims are reviewed by real people, and a pattern of unfounded claims can cost you the ability to make new ones.
:::

## The venue is already claimed

If a venue already has a verified owner, the claim button is replaced by an ownership row that reads **Owned by {owner} · Request transfer**. The owner's name links to their own page — an organisation or a person — so you can see who runs the venue before doing anything. When the owner has no public page of their own, the row simply reads **Verified owner** and names nobody.

Use **Request transfer** if you believe you're the rightful operator. You'll be asked for a reason (at least a few sentences) and can attach supporting documents. The same action is always available from the **⋯** menu in the action bar at the bottom of the venue page.

A dispute is never resolved automatically. Our team reviews it, and the current owner is notified that a dispute has been filed. If the dispute is upheld, ownership transfers to you and the previous owner loses access and is notified of the change.

## Requesting verification for a venue you already manage

Claiming and verification are related but not the same thing. If the venue is already in your **Places** list — you created it, or you're an admin of the organisation that owns it — there's nothing to claim, but it still needs to be **verified** before it enters the public directory or can accept booking requests.

Ask for verification from wherever you happen to be; all three routes open the same dialog and create the same request:

1. **From the venue's details page** — open the venue and find the **Verification** panel in the settings column beside the form (below the form on a narrow screen). It shows the current status and what that status means, and **Request Verification** sits at the bottom of it. Anything standing in the way — unsaved edits, a missing street address — is listed directly above the button, and the button stays greyed out until all of it is cleared.
2. **From the Places list** — open the **⋯** menu on the venue's row and choose **Request verification**.
3. **From the venue's Verification page** — in the sidebar's **Venue** group.

In the dialog, drop in one to five files (PDF, JPEG, or PNG, up to 10 MB each) showing that your group operates the venue — a business licence, lease, or utility bill. Only the business name and venue address need to be readable; redact anything else. Then click **Submit for review**.

:::tip
Save your changes first. Reviewers judge the saved venue, so an unsaved name or address would make your request unreviewable — the details page won't let you start one until you've saved, and if you begin from the Places list instead, the dialog says so and holds the submit button. Either way you're told what's missing, such as a street address.
:::

Afterwards the venue's **Verification** page is where you follow along: it shows your place in the review queue, tells you when a reviewer has picked the request up, and carries the reviewer's notes if the request is rejected. You can **Withdraw** a request while it's still waiting, which hands the venue straight back to unverified so you can fix something and ask again. If a request is rejected, resubmitting offers to reuse the documents you already uploaded, so you don't have to attach them a second time to fix a one-line note.

## What can go wrong

- **You can't submit — the button is greyed out.** The dialog lists why directly above the drop zone: unsaved edits on the details form, or missing venue data such as a street address or city. Fix what it names and the button arms.
- **No claim button appears.** The venue may already have a verified owner (you'll see the **Owned by … · Request transfer** row), or a claim may already be under review by someone else (you'll see **Claim pending review**). Only one claim can be open on a venue at a time; if you believe you're the operator, use **Request transfer**.
- **Your email domain isn't accepted.** Public email providers are rejected, and the address domain must match the venue's website. If neither fits, use the document method instead.
- **You can't edit the venue yet.** Editing unlocks only once a claim is verified. Until then you'll see the pending badge and read-only details.
