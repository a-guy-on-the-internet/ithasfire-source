A place page can exist on Ithas Fire before its operator ever signs up — anyone can create a basic listing, and events can point at it. If you run the place, claiming its page lets you correct its details, get it into the public directory, and manage it under your own account or organisation.

## Before you start

- You need an Ithas Fire account and must be signed in.
- Decide who should own the page: **you personally**, or an **organisation** you administer. You can only claim on behalf of an organisation where you're an admin.
- Have proof that you operate the place ready — access to an email address at the place's website domain, or a document such as a utility bill, lease, or business registration.

## Claiming a place

1. Open the place's page. If nobody has claimed it yet, you'll see a **Claim this place** button in the row of buttons under its name. If you're signed out, pressing it asks you to sign in first.
2. Choose who is claiming — **Myself**, or an organisation you administer.
3. Choose how to prove you operate the place. Three methods are offered:
   - **Account email** — the domain of your verified account email matches the place's website (for example, your account email ends in `@thecornertavern.com` and the place's website is `thecornertavern.com`).
   - **Email code** — we send a six-digit code to an address you enter at the place's website domain. Enter the code to confirm you control a mailbox there.
   - **Upload document** — attach one to five files (PDF, JPEG, or PNG, up to 10 MB each) that show you operate the place.
4. Submit. You'll either see **Claim submitted** (your claim is now waiting for review) or, in the fast-track case described below, **You now own this place**.

:::tip
Free and public email providers (Gmail, Outlook, and similar) can't be used for the **Account email** or **Email code** methods — they don't prove any link to the place. Use an address at the place's own domain, or choose the document method.
:::

### When a claim verifies instantly

If your **Account email** domain matches a place website that we imported from a trusted source, the claim skips review and verifies immediately — the wizard confirms **You now own this place**. Every other route (an email code, an uploaded document, or a website that was added by an unverified listing) stays pending until our team reviews it. This is deliberate: a place's website can be set by whoever first created the page, so a domain match alone is a signal, not proof.

## While your claim is pending

A pending claim records that you've claimed the place, but it does **not** yet let you edit the place's details, and the place stays out of the public directory until it's verified. The place page shows a **Claim pending review** badge in the meantime.

To track a claim you've submitted, open your account settings and find the **Place claims** section. Each claim shows its status — **Pending**, **In review**, **Approved**, or **Rejected** — along with the date it was reviewed and any note the reviewer left.

When a reviewer **approves** your claim, the place becomes verified: you can edit its details and it's eligible for the public directory. If a claim is **rejected**, the place returns to its unclaimed state and the reviewer's note explains why — you're free to correct the issue and claim again.

:::warning
You can hold up to 20 pending claims at once. Submit claims only for places you actually operate; claims are reviewed by real people, and a pattern of unfounded claims can cost you the ability to make new ones.
:::

## If moderators wrote the page before you

Until someone claims a place, community moderators can write its page: the bio, images, page sections and theme. Its name and address aren't part of the page. While their work is on it, visitors see a **Community-maintained** line on the page. An image a moderator adds stays off the public page until it passes our image review.

Approving your claim leaves their work as it is. Until you decide what to do with it, a notice sits at the top of the page editor (**Edit page content**) and of the **Public page** section of the place's Settings. It lists who made changes, how many, and when. Choose one:

- **Keep it** — the page stays as it is and becomes yours. The community line comes off the public page. A moderator's image that is still in review stays hidden until it passes.
- **Clear it** — empties the bio, avatar and banner, and puts the main page and theme back to the starting template. It also removes any main-page edits you made yourself since the claim, so choose before you start editing. Other sections, such as memberships, stay as they are. You're asked to confirm first.

## The place is already claimed

If a place already has a verified owner, the claim button is replaced by an ownership row that reads **Owned by {owner} · Request transfer**. The owner's name links to their own page — an organisation or a person — so you can see who runs the place before doing anything. When the owner has no public page of their own, the row simply reads **Verified owner** and names nobody.

Use **Request transfer** if you believe you're the rightful operator. You'll be asked for a reason (at least a few sentences) and can attach supporting documents. The same action is always available from the **⋯** menu at the end of the row of buttons under the place's name.

A dispute is never resolved automatically. Our team reviews it, and the current owner is notified that a dispute has been filed. If the dispute is upheld, ownership transfers to you and the previous owner loses access and is notified of the change.

## Requesting verification for a place you already manage

Claiming and verification are related but not the same thing. If the place is already in your **Places** list — you created it, or you're an admin of the organisation that owns it — there's nothing to claim, but it still needs to be **verified** before it enters the public directory or can accept booking requests.

Ask for verification from either screen; both use the same upload form and create the same request:

1. **From the place's Settings page** — open the place (**Settings** is the first item in the sidebar's **Place** group) and go to the **Verification** section. It shows the current status, and **Request Verification** sits beside it. Anything standing in the way — unsaved edits, a missing street address — is listed under the status, and the button stays greyed out until all of it is cleared. Pressing it opens the upload form right there in the section.
2. **From the Places list** — open the **⋯** menu on the place's row and choose **Request verification**. This opens the same form in a dialog.

Drop in one to five files (PDF, JPEG, or PNG, up to 10 MB each) showing that your group operates the place — a business licence, lease, or utility bill. Only the business name and place address need to be readable; redact anything else. Then click **Submit for review**.

:::tip
Save your changes first. Reviewers judge the saved place, so an unsaved name or address would make your request unreviewable — the Settings page won't let you start one until you've pressed **Save changes**, and if you begin from the Places list instead, the dialog says so and holds the submit button. Either way you're told what's missing, such as a street address. Verification has its own submit button on purpose: **Save changes** saves your edits, but it never sends a verification request.
:::

Afterwards the **Verification** section of the place's Settings page is where you follow along: it shows your place in the review queue, tells you when a reviewer has picked the request up, and carries the reviewer's notes if the request is rejected. You can **Withdraw** a request while it's still waiting, which hands the place straight back to unverified so you can fix something and ask again. If a request is rejected, resubmitting offers to reuse the documents you already uploaded, so you don't have to attach them a second time to fix a one-line note.

## What can go wrong

- **You can't submit — the button is greyed out.** The form lists why: unsaved edits on the Settings page, or missing place data such as a street address or city. Fix what it names and the button arms.
- **No claim button appears.** The place may already have a verified owner (you'll see the **Owned by … · Request transfer** row), or a claim may already be under review by someone else (you'll see **Claim pending review**). Only one claim can be open on a place at a time; if you believe you're the operator, use **Request transfer**.
- **Your email domain isn't accepted.** Public email providers are rejected, and the address domain must match the place's website. If neither fits, use the document method instead.
- **You can't edit the place yet.** Editing unlocks only once a claim is verified. Until then you'll see the pending badge and read-only details.
