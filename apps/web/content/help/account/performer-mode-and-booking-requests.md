Booking requests let you pitch a night directly to places on Ithas Fire, for your own act or for a bill of acts you're putting together. This article covers performer mode, sending a request from a place's page, listing the acts on the bill, and tracking or withdrawing requests you've sent.

## Turn on performer mode

1. Open [Settings → Performer](/account/settings/performer).
2. Flip the **Performer mode** switch on.

Performer mode is what lets you put your own act on the bill of a request. You can send a request to play without it: a promoter pitching a night for other acts doesn't need it, and leaves themselves off the bill. Turning the switch off later hides performer features but keeps your data, so nothing is lost if you change your mind.

Booking for a band or group? Performer mode is per-identity: a group admin can turn it on for the group in the group's own settings. The request form lets you pick who to **Send as**: you, or a group you're an admin of.

Want people to book **you**? Two options, and they stack:

- **Contact details** — add a booking section with your email, phone, or a booking link to your public page in the page editor. Visitors see a **Booking** button at the top of your profile that jumps straight to it.
- **Booking requests to your act** — in the same Performer section, switch on **Open to booking requests**. Organizers on Ithas Fire then see a **Request to book** button on your public page and can send you a proper request — dates, an optional offer, sometimes a specific event. You'll be notified, and everything lands under **Requests to you** in [Settings → Booking requests](/account/settings/booking-requests), where you accept (creating a draft agreement to negotiate terms) or decline. The switch is off until you turn it on, and turning it off stops new requests without touching existing ones.

Requests sent to a **group** you manage don't come here — they wait in that group's admin area under **Bookings**, on the **To you as an act** tab, alongside the group's other booking work. The group's own **Performer** and **Booking defaults** switches stay in its settings.

:::tip
You don't have to set this up in advance. If you open **Play here** without performer mode on, **I'm on the bill** is switched off and a **Turn on performer mode** button sits beside it. Renting or using a space never needs performer mode.
:::

## Send a request to a place

The **Play here** button appears on the public pages of places that are verified and currently accepting booking requests — near the top of the page, under the place's name. On a phone it is also the large button in the middle of the bar at the bottom of the screen. If a place hasn't opted in, you won't see it. Places that also rent or lend their spaces show a second button, **Rent or use a space** — see [Rent or use a space](#rent-or-use-a-space) below.

1. Open the place's public page and click **Play here**. (Signed out? You'll be sent through sign-in and back.)
2. If you're an admin of a group, choose who to **Send as**.
3. Optionally list the acts on the night under **The bill** — see [Put acts on the bill](#put-acts-on-the-bill) below.
4. Write your **Message** — introduce yourself and your act (at least 10 characters).
5. Pick 1–3 **Preferred dates**. Dates must be today or later.
6. Optionally add your **Expected draw** (how many people you expect to bring), a **Genre**, and up to 3 **Links** — full URLs starting with `http(s)://`.
7. Click **Send request**.

Near the top of the form, when the place has enough door history, you'll see a line like **"usually 25–100 people checked in"** — a rough sense of the room's typical crowd. It counts people actually scanned in at the door, not tickets sold. Someone who came in still counts if their ticket was refunded later, but a scan the door staff undid straight away doesn't count.

You'll see a confirmation that the request was sent, and the place will review it.

You can have **one open request per place** at a time. Trying to send another while one is still open shows "You already have an open request with this place."

## Put acts on the bill

A request to play can say who's on the night. **The bill** sits in the **Your act** section of the form, and it's optional: leave it empty and the request goes out from you alone.

- **I'm on the bill** puts your own act first, as the headliner. It needs performer mode on for whoever you're sending as. Promoting a night you won't play? Leave it off.
- To add an act, start typing under **Name**. People and groups on Ithas Fire show up as you type; pick one and they'll be asked to join. People only appear if their profile page is public. If the act isn't on Ithas Fire, type the name, add an optional **Link**, and it goes on the bill **By name**.
- Choose a **Role** for each act (Performer, DJ, Host and so on, or **Other** with your own **Role name**), then click **Add to the bill**.
- **Move up** and **Move down** change the order. The act in first place reads **Headlining**. The bin removes an act.

A bill holds up to 12 acts, yours included. Acts you pick from search aren't on the night yet: each one gets an invitation and answers it themselves, and the place sees who has joined and who is still invited. There's a daily limit on how many acts you can invite; past it, add the rest by name or send the request the next day.

## If someone puts you on a bill

When another act or a promoter puts you on a bill, you're notified with a link to the request. The page shows who sent it, the place, the preferred dates, your spot and the bill so far. Click **Count me in** to join, or **I'll pass**, with an optional reason. A group admin can answer for the group.

Nothing is booked when you join: the place and the sender still have to agree the terms. You can **Leave the bill** until the night is booked. Passing is final for that request. If you join after the night is booked and the event is still upcoming, you go straight onto its lineup — see [Lineups you're on](/help/account/lineups-youre-on).

## Request through a place's own website

Some places put a **Request to book** button on their own websites. It opens the same kind of booking form, and you don't need an Ithas Fire account to use it — just your name, email, act or band name, 1–3 preferred dates, and a pitch, with optional details like genre, expected draw, and links. A short verification check runs before **Send request**, and the place replies to the email address you gave. As on the platform, you can have one open request per place at a time.

If you've booked with Ithas Fire before, click **Log in** next to "Booked with Ithas Fire before?" — a popup signs you in (if you're already signed in on ithasfire.com, it completes without re-entering anything) and the form switches to your account: pick who to **Submit as** (yourself or a group you manage) and your saved booking defaults are prefilled. Requests sent this way behave exactly like requests sent from the place's Ithas Fire page, including tracking below.

:::tip
If your browser blocks the sign-in popup, allow popups for the place's site and click **Log in** again — or just use the guest form; you can attach your account later if the place accepts.
:::

## Claim a booking you requested without an account

If you sent a request through a place's website without logging in and the place accepts it, you'll get an email inviting you to finalise the booking. The link takes you to a claim page on Ithas Fire:

1. **Sign in** — or create an account if you don't have one yet.
2. If performer mode isn't on, turn it on with the one-click **Enable performer mode** button.
3. Choose who you're **claiming as** — yourself, or a group you manage. The draft agreement is created under that name.
4. Click **Claim booking**.

That's the moment the **draft agreement** between you and the place is created — **Open the agreement** to negotiate the terms (see **Accepted? Open the agreement** below), and the request appears among your sent booking requests.

The claim link is **single-use** and expires **30 days** after it's sent. If it has expired, or you see "This link is no longer active" (it was already used, or a newer invite email replaced it), ask the place to resend the invite — they can do that from their booking inbox, and you'll get a fresh link by email.

## Rent or use a space

Not every request is a gig. A place can open its rooms for hire, or lend them for a meeting, a reading, a film night, a rehearsal. When it does, its page shows **Rent or use a space** next to **Play here**. You don't need performer mode for this — anyone with an account can ask.

1. Click **Rent or use a space**. If the place offers both, you're asked **What are you asking for?** — **Rent a space** or **Use a space**. Renting is a paid hire; using is the place lending you the room.
2. Under **The space**, pick the **Calendar**. Only calendars that take your kind of request are listed, and a place that runs just one shows its name instead of asking you to choose. Any rooms on that calendar are listed underneath so you can see what it covers — you don't pick one. The place picks the room when it accepts.
3. Under **When**, choose the **Date** from the place's calendar. Days are marked **Free**, **Part booked** or **Unavailable**, and picking one shows that day's opening hours and anything already booked on it. Then set **Start** and **End** yourself — the calendar tells you what's open, it doesn't hand you a slot. Times are in the place's local time; a booking that runs past midnight is marked as ending the next day.
4. Under **What it's for**, say in a line **What is it** and give your **Expected attendance**.
5. Add a contact number under **Contact** if you'd like the place to have one, then send.

If the place has published a rate for the calendar you chose, it's shown on the form as **Rent: … per booking**, and it's paid to the place directly if they confirm. Ithas Fire doesn't take or hold that money.

Your request lands in the place's Bookings inbox like any other. If they say yes, the place chooses who runs the event. Most rentals and uses are yours: a draft event appears in your own admin, on the date you asked for, ready for you to finish and publish. Your own payout terms apply to any tickets you sell on it. The place can instead keep the event on its own account, in which case it lives in their admin and they run the night.

:::tip
Only places that have claimed their page on Ithas Fire take requests. If a place hasn't, you won't see **Play here** or **Rent or use a space** on its page; contact them another way.
:::

## Track, withdraw, and statuses

Everything you've sent lives under [Settings → Booking requests](/account/settings/booking-requests), newest first. Requests sent on behalf of a group are labelled **Sent as {group name}**.

| Status        | Meaning                                                 |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Pending**   | Sent, but the place hasn't opened it yet                |
| **Viewed**    | The place has opened your request                       |
| **Accepted**  | The place accepted your request                         |
| **Declined**  | The place passed on this request                        |
| **Withdrawn** | You withdrew the request                                |
| **Expired**   | The place didn't respond within 14 days                 |

Open requests (Pending or Viewed) show a **Withdraw** button. Withdrawing closes the request — and since the one-open-request limit only counts open requests, you're then free to send a fresh one if your availability changes.

## Talk to the place, and follow the booking

Every request you send is also a message thread. **Conversation** on the row opens it, and it stays open at every status — a declined or expired request is still somewhere to ask what happened with that Friday. Messages cap at 4,000 characters. The thread doesn't refresh on its own; **Refresh conversation** (the ⟳ button in the heading) pulls in anything new.

A **2 unread** badge sits on the row, just before that button, whenever the place has written something you haven't read. Opening the request clears it.

Between the messages you'll find the booking's timeline — quiet lines Ithas Fire writes itself each time the booking moves. Your request being sent, opened by the place, accepted or declined with their reason, or expiring. Then the agreement: drafted, sent for review, accepted, declined, cancelled by the place with their reason, pulled back to edit, reopened, finalised. Then the event: created, with the date it's for, and every later change to its title, start or end time, location, calendar, space, or time zone — a change of date spelled out as a move.

Agreement lines carry a **View agreement** link. Event lines don't carry a link to the event: it lives in the place's admin area, which you have no access to, so you see what changed and when without a link that would only bounce you.

:::tip
Timeline lines never count as unread. The badge means the place has actually written to you, not that the booking's paperwork moved.
:::

:::warning
Requests expire automatically **14 days** after you send them if the place doesn't respond. An expired request can't be reopened, but you can send a new one.
:::

## Being on hold

A place that's interested but not ready to commit can put a **hold** on a date for you. You'll hear about it — in-app, by email and by push — with the place, the date and your spot in line: **First in line**, or **2nd in line**, **3rd**, and so on. The position is the whole of what you're told about the line. You never see who else is in it, and they never see you; if the place moves you up, you hear the new position and nothing more.

A hold is not a booking. Nothing is agreed until the place confirms it, and they can release it, in which case you're told and your request stays open. If the place confirms a different date for you instead, any holds you had on other dates are released along with it.

Sometimes another act wants the night you're first in line for. The place then asks you to **keep or release** the hold by a deadline — 24 hours is usual, and the place can give you longer. Keep means you're taking the date; release means you're stepping aside and the next act moves up. While your reply clock runs, the place can't give the date to another act. If you don't answer by the deadline, the hold lapses on its own and the line moves up without you; your request is still open either way. If you're second in line when the first hold is challenged, you're told when you'll hear, and then whether the first hold stood or the date moved to you.

When the place confirms your hold, the date is booked: a draft event is created and the place finishes the details with you through the agreement and the conversation. If the date goes to another act, you're told the hold was released — without being told to whom — and you can propose another date in the thread. The place can also simply put a show on the date: if they publish an event there that didn't come from your request, your hold is released, you're told the place scheduled an event on that date, and your request stays open.

## Accepted? Open the agreement

When a place accepts, the request shows a **View agreement** button — acceptance creates a **draft agreement** between you and the place. The place or organizer sets the terms: title, proposed dates, an optional guarantee, notes, and **payout lines** (your share of the takings). The money terms are written in **The deal** on the agreement page. When they use **Send for review**, you accept or decline with a reason — there's no counter on the agreement itself; if something needs to change, say so in the conversation and they pull it back to keep editing. The place can also cancel the agreement outright, at any point before an event is finalised onto it; you'll see their reason in the conversation, and a cancelled agreement stays cancelled. Once the agreement is accepted its payout terms are finalised onto the event — that's what settlement pays out against.

If you're the payee on a line, you don't have to take it as written. Alongside **Confirm your line** you can **Counter** — enter a **Proposed share %** and a short **Note** (shared with the agreement parties), then **Send counter** — or **Decline** the line. A counter is an ask, not an edit: the parties see it on the line and can apply it from the draft editor. The agreement can't be finalised onto an event while any line is countered or declined, so an accepted agreement may go back to draft (**Reopen draft**) to renegotiate — editing your line's terms restarts your confirmation.

## What can go wrong

- **"This place isn't accepting booking requests right now."** — the place switched off requests after you opened the page. Try again later or contact them another way.
- **"You already have an open request with this place."** — withdraw your existing request first if you want to send a different pitch.
- **"You don't have permission to send as this performer."** — only group admins can send requests on a group's behalf.
- **"Turn on performer mode to put your own act on the bill, or take yourself off it."** — whoever you're sending as doesn't have performer mode on. Turn it on, or switch off **I'm on the bill**.
- **"One of the acts you tagged can't be invited. Remove it and add it by name instead."** — that person or group can't be invited from a request. Adding them by name still lists them on the bill.
- **"You've invited as many acts as you can today."** — you've hit the daily invitation limit. Add the rest by name, or send the request tomorrow.
- **"This link has expired."** on the claim page — claim links last 30 days. Ask the place to resend the invite from their booking inbox.
- **"This link is no longer active."** — the claim link was already used, or a newer invite email replaced it. Check your inbox for the latest link; if the claim already went through, the booking is under your sent requests.

To learn what places see on their side, read [receive booking requests from performers](/help/organising-events/booking-requests).
